What is business operations support?

Business operations support is the work of keeping the day-to-day running of a business organized: who is doing what, by when, and following which steps. It covers project coordination, task tracking, written procedures (SOPs) and fixing the processes behind problems that keep coming back.

It differs from executive assistant support, which manages one leader’s calendar and inbox, and from administrative support, which handles documents and data. Wondering how this compares with managing operations in-house? Read business operations vs operations management.

Sound familiar?

If your business works only because a couple of people remember how, it is not broken. It is fragile.

  • Work depends on one or two people remembering how it is done. When they are away or overloaded, things stall.
  • There are no written procedures. Every new task, and every new hire, starts from scratch.
  • Tasks live in inboxes, chats and memory. Nobody can see in one place what is open, due or stuck.
  • The same problems keep coming back. Nobody has the time to find and fix the process behind them.
  • Requests and handoffs have no clear owner. Work bounces between people and slows down.

What business operations support includes

Project coordination

Tasks, owners and deadlines stay visible, so projects keep moving between meetings and nothing waits for someone to remember it.

Task tracking

One place to see what is open, what is due and what is stuck, so you stop asking for status updates.

SOP creation and documentation

The way your business does things is written down in plain language, so it can be repeated, shared and handed off to a new hire.

Process improvement

Repeating problems are traced to the process behind them and fixed once, instead of being firefought every week.

Workflow organization

Requests, approvals and handoffs follow a clear route with a named owner, instead of living in inboxes and chats.

One place to see what is open, due and stuck

Every task gets an owner and a status in one tracker, so you can see where things stand without asking anyone.

What your first 30 days look like

This is a typical pattern. The pace follows how much you want to hand over.

  • Week 1: access to your tools and a map of how two or three key processes run today.
  • Week 2: the biggest slowdown fixed and the new route agreed with the people involved.
  • Week 3: the first SOPs written in plain language and shared with your team.
  • Week 4: tasks in one tracker, a review of what is working and an agreed ongoing rhythm.

Common mistakes when organizing operations

  • Writing SOPs nobody uses. Procedures that do not match how work really happens get ignored. Start from how the work is done today.
  • Rebuilding everything at once. Fix the step that hurts most first, then move on.
  • Buying software before the process is clear. A tool cannot fix a process nobody has defined.
  • Leaving tasks without an owner. If everyone owns it, nobody does.
  • Treating it as a one-off project. Processes drift. A short regular review keeps them working.

Questions about business operations

Can you improve the processes we already have?

Yes. I start by mapping how work actually flows today, find the step where things slow down or get lost, and fix that first, instead of rebuilding everything.

Do you create SOPs from scratch?

Yes. I write them in plain language from how you already do the work, so your team can follow, repeat and hand them off.

Do I need to change the tools I use?

Not necessarily. I work in the tools you already have where I can, and suggest a simple tracker if you do not have one.

How do I know if my business needs operations support?

If tasks live in inboxes and memory, the same problems keep returning, or things stall when one person is away, it likely does.

What is the difference between business operations support and administrative support?

Business operations support organizes how work gets done: tasks, owners, procedures and workflows. Administrative support handles the work itself, such as data entry, documents and reports.

How long does it take to see results?

You usually feel the difference once the first tracker and the first SOP are in place, often within the first month. It depends on how much you hand over.

How quickly can we start?

After the discovery call we agree on the processes to look at first and a start date. I begin with the area that hurts most.

How fast do you respond?

Same day, including weekends, in your time zone.
