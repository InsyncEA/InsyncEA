What is executive assistant support?

Executive assistant support covers the day-to-day work that keeps a busy leader organized: deciding which meetings make the calendar, keeping the inbox under control, preparing for meetings, arranging travel and making sure follow-ups happen. It is the work a virtual executive assistant does, and today it is often delivered remotely, in your time zone.

It differs from general administrative support. Administrative support focuses on documents, data and reports, and you can read about it under remote administrative support. Executive assistant support is built around one person’s priorities and time. If your business processes need organizing too, see business operations support.

Sound familiar?

If you recognize two or three of these, you are not disorganized. You are doing a second job on top of your first.

  • Your calendar fills up with the wrong meetings. Time for real work shrinks while requests you could have declined or delegated keep landing.
  • Important emails sit unanswered. Urgent messages get handled first, so the quieter but important ones wait too long.
  • Follow-ups slip after calls and meetings. Next steps are agreed in the room and forgotten by the end of the week.
  • Scheduling takes endless back-and-forth. Finding a time across several people can eat a surprising part of your day.
  • Travel and meeting prep land at the last minute. Itineraries, agendas and materials get pulled together in a rush.

What executive assistant support includes

Calendar management and executive scheduling

Meetings are booked around your priorities, with conflicts, buffers and time zones handled before they become your problem. Focus time is protected, not squeezed in.

Inbox management

Messages are sorted, replies are drafted or sent within the guidelines you set, and what needs your attention is surfaced first.

Meeting coordination

Invitations, agendas, materials and follow-up notes are handled, so meetings start prepared and end with next steps.

Travel coordination

Itineraries, bookings and changes are organized in one place, with the details ready before you need them.

Follow-ups and reminders

Open loops are tracked until they close, so nothing waits on your memory.

How inbox management works

A typical triage sorts every message into one of three lanes: needs your decision, drafted for your approval, or handled and filed. You look at the first lane every day. The rest you only review.

How we work together, step by step

  1. Discovery call

    We talk through what is taking your time, your priorities and how you like things done.

  2. Set access and rules

    You decide which inboxes, calendars and folders I can see, and what I can send or accept without asking.

  3. First handoff

    I start with one or two high-impact areas, usually the calendar and inbox, and confirm your preferences.

  4. Weekly rhythm

    A short weekly check-in covers decisions, upcoming travel and open follow-ups. In between, I work independently and flag only what needs you.

What your first 30 days look like

This is a typical pattern. The pace follows how much you want to hand over.

  • Week 1: access, priorities and a first pass at the calendar and inbox.
  • Week 2: written rules, templates for common replies and a regular scheduling routine.
  • Week 3: more areas handed over, such as travel and meeting prep, with follow-ups tracked in one place.
  • Week 4: a review of what is working, adjusted rules and an agreed ongoing rhythm.

Common mistakes when delegating to an assistant

  • Delegating without rules. Agree what can be sent, accepted or declined without asking.
  • Handing over everything on day one. Start with the calendar and inbox, then add more.
  • No stated priorities. Without a clear list of what matters, scheduling defaults to first come, first served.
  • Skipping the weekly check-in. Small misunderstandings grow when nobody reviews them.
  • Keeping context to yourself. Short notes on people and projects save you repeating yourself.

Questions about executive assistant support

Do you work in my time zone?

I work in your time zone. Most of my clients are on US Eastern Time, and I adjust my working hours to match yours, so you get live support during your business day. Much of the work, like inbox triage and follow-ups, does not need live overlap.

How do you manage confidential information?

We agree on access and confidentiality up front, on the discovery call. You decide what I can see and do, such as which inboxes, calendars or folders, and I keep to it.

Can you send emails on my behalf?

Yes, within guidelines we set together. I can draft replies for your approval or send routine ones directly, and I flag anything that needs your decision.

What is the difference between executive assistant support and administrative support?

Executive assistant support is built around one leader’s priorities and time: calendar, inbox, meetings, travel and follow-ups. Administrative support focuses on documents, data entry and reports.

How quickly can we start?

After the discovery call we agree access, priorities and a start date. I begin with the calendar and inbox, then add more areas as you get comfortable.

How fast do you respond?

Same day, including weekends.

Do I need to be a founder to use executive assistant support?

No. I support founders, executives, business owners and their teams.
