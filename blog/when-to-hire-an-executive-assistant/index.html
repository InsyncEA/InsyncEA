When to Hire an Executive Assistant: 9 Clear Signs You're Ready

August 3, 2026 · 7 min read

If you've typed "when to hire an executive assistant" into Google at 11pm while replying to emails you should have delegated weeks ago, you already know the answer somewhere in the back of your mind. The harder part is knowing for sure, and knowing you're not jumping the gun.

Most founders and executives don't hire an EA too early. They hire one about six months too late, after burnout, missed opportunities, or one too many double-booked meetings force the decision. This guide walks through the concrete signs it's time, what an executive assistant actually takes off your plate, and how to make the transition smoothly.

What Does an Executive Assistant Actually Do?

Before diving into the signs, it helps to be clear on scope. A good executive assistant isn't just someone who answers your emails. Depending on the arrangement, an EA typically manages:

  • Calendar and scheduling, including protecting focus time
  • Inbox triage and email management
  • Travel planning and itineraries
  • Meeting prep, agendas, and follow-ups
  • Light project coordination and research
  • Vendor and client communication on your behalf

The goal isn't to replace your judgment, it's to remove everything before the judgment call so you only spend time on decisions that actually need you.

9 Signs It's Time to Hire an Executive Assistant

1. Your Calendar Runs You, Instead of the Other Way Around

If meetings get booked without your input, back-to-back with no buffer, and you regularly show up unprepared because you didn't have time to review the agenda, your calendar has become reactive rather than strategic. That's an EA's first job to fix.

2. You're Answering Emails That Don't Require Your Judgment

Confirming a meeting time, forwarding a document, or replying "sounds good" to a routine request are all tasks that don't need the highest-paid person in the business doing them. If your inbox is full of these, you're paying founder-level rates for admin-level work.

3. Travel Planning Eats an Entire Afternoon

Flights, hotels, ground transport, timezone math, booking a single multi-city trip can burn hours you don't have. An EA who knows your preferences can do this in a fraction of the time, and get it right the first time.

4. You've Missed a Deadline or Dropped a Follow-Up

One missed follow-up because "it slipped through the cracks" is often the moment founders finally admit they need support. If it's happened more than once, that's your answer.

5. You're Working Evenings and Weekends Just to "Catch Up"

If your actual working hours are consumed by meetings and your real work happens after 8pm, that's not a productivity problem, it's a delegation problem.

6. Your Business Has Grown, But Your Support Hasn't

Revenue, headcount, and client volume all scale. Admin support often doesn't, until it becomes the bottleneck. If your business looks nothing like it did a year ago but you're still handling the same admin load yourself, it's time.

7. You Avoid Tasks You Know You Should Be Doing

Strategic planning, relationship building, and fundraising conversations are the high-value work that keeps getting pushed down the list because low-value admin keeps filling your day first.

8. You've Thought About Hiring One Before

If this isn't the first time you've searched for this, that instinct is worth listening to. Founders usually know before they act.

9. You Can Calculate What Your Time Is Actually Worth

If your time is worth more per hour than an EA's, and you're still spending hours a week on tasks an EA could do, the math already answers the question for you. Robert Half's salary guide is a useful benchmark if you want real numbers to run that comparison against.

Executive Assistant vs. Virtual Assistant: Which Do You Need?

A common next question is whether you need a full executive assistant or a general virtual assistant. Broadly:

  • Virtual assistants typically handle discrete, task-based work, data entry, basic research, simple scheduling.
  • Executive assistants operate more strategically, they understand your priorities, manage relationships on your behalf, and make judgment calls within boundaries you set.

If you need someone who can think a step ahead of you, not just execute a task list, you're looking for an EA.

Making the Switch Without Wasting Money

The biggest mistake founders make when hiring an EA isn't hiring too early, it's hiring without a plan. Before you bring someone on:

  1. Track your week. For five days, note every task that isn't strategic decision-making. That list becomes your EA's job description.
  2. Start with the highest-friction task. Usually calendar or inbox. Handing over one system first builds trust faster than handing over everything at once.
  3. Document as you go. Even a rough SOP for how you like meetings scheduled or emails triaged saves weeks of back-and-forth.
  4. Decide part-time vs. full-time. Many founders start with 10-15 hours a week and scale up once the working relationship proves itself.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many signs do I need before I should hire an executive assistant?
You don't need all nine. If three or more sound familiar, it's a strong signal that hiring an EA has become a necessity rather than a nice-to-have.

What's the difference between an executive assistant and a virtual assistant?
Virtual assistants typically handle discrete, task-based work like data entry and basic scheduling. Executive assistants operate more strategically, managing relationships and making judgment calls within boundaries you set.

Should I hire an executive assistant part-time or full-time?
Many founders start with 10-15 hours a week and scale up once the working relationship proves itself, rather than committing to full-time from day one.

The Real Cost of Waiting

As Harvard Business Review has noted, leaders often stay tangled in their teams' work long past the point it's actually helping. Every week you delay hiring an EA is a week spent doing low-value work instead of the work that actually grows your business. The cost of hiring isn't just the EA's rate, it's what you could have been doing with those hours instead.

If you're recognizing three or more of the signs above, the question isn't really "when should I hire an executive assistant." It's "what's it costing me not to."
