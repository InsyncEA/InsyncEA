Executive Research Support: Turning Scattered Information into Clear Summaries for Decisions

Executives make better decisions when information is organized and easy to scan. I conducted research to support executive decision-making and presented the findings in clear, usable formats.

The business challenge

Useful information is rarely in one place. Sources vary in quality, and a decision-maker rarely has time to read everything.

My approach

  • Clarified the question the research needed to answer.
  • Gathered information from relevant sources.
  • Organized findings around what mattered to the decision.
  • Summarized the results in clear formats, such as concise summaries and comparisons.
  • Highlighted key points and open questions.

What a decision-ready research summary includes

A good summary lets a busy leader understand the situation in minutes. A typical layout has:

  • The question: what the research is meant to answer.
  • Key findings: the points that matter most, up front.
  • A comparison: options side by side, when a choice is involved.
  • Open questions: what is still uncertain or unknown.

How to brief research support

  1. State the decision, not just the topic, so the research stays focused.
  2. Set the deadline and how much detail you want.
  3. Say what format helps you most, such as a one-page summary or a comparison table.
  4. Mention sources to use or avoid, if you have preferences.
  5. Ask for open questions to be flagged, so gaps are visible.

The outcome

Executives had organized, summarized information to work from, which made it easier to weigh options without reading every source.

Signs you need research support

  • Decisions wait because nobody has time to gather the information.
  • You read long documents to find a few relevant points.
  • Comparing options means collecting details from many places.

Questions about executive research support

What is executive research support?

It is research done on behalf of a decision-maker: clarifying the question, gathering information from relevant sources, organizing it around the decision and presenting it in clear, scannable formats.

What did the research support cover in this case?

Clarifying the question the research needed to answer, gathering information from relevant sources, organizing findings around the decision, and summarizing results in formats such as concise summaries and comparisons, with key points and open questions highlighted.

How long should a research summary be?

Short enough to scan in minutes. A common approach is a one-page summary of the key findings, with supporting detail kept separate for anyone who wants it.

How do you deal with sources of different quality?

Sources are chosen for relevance, and the summary highlights open questions, so you can see where information is uncertain or incomplete.
